A ‘group’ is a set of identical items, all of which are directly adjacent to at least one identical item, either horizontally or vertically (not diagonally).

In each diagram, the largest such group is identified (e.g. in diagram one there is a group of five circles; the next largest being a group of four triangles).

All items in this connected group are then replaced, according to these rules: circles become squares, squares become triangles and triangles become circles.

Once all items have become identical in this way (as in diagram four), a new pattern begins, which will follow the same rules.

The largest group in diagram five is a set of five squares, which will thus be replaced with triangles.

Other squares are not affected as they don't belong to the connected group of squares.
